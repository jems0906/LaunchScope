# Decision Brief

## Initiative

Introduce a fictional game-discovery module to eligible households. The control is the existing fictional discovery experience. This educational blueprint uses synthetic data and does not describe Netflix methods, internal systems, or actual outcomes.

## Decision to support

Should the accountable product stakeholder continue, revise, or stop offering the module to eligible households after the fixed evaluation window?

The study can estimate the effect of offering the module on the prespecified qualified-game-session outcome. It cannot establish customer satisfaction, retention, financial value, or creative quality.

## Intake record

| Field | Prespecified value |
| --- | --- |
| Accountable stakeholder role | Content & Games Product Lead |
| Proposed decision | Continue, revise, or stop the offer |
| Alternatives | Existing discovery only; limited redesign and retest; no rollout |
| Eligible population | Households meeting product eligibility rules recorded before assignment |
| Timing | One fixed 28-day observation window after assignment; no early stopping |
| Cost of a false positive | Household experience or playback quality may worsen while the initiative is continued |
| Cost of a false negative | A useful discovery experience may be delayed or abandoned |
| Decision owner | Stakeholder, informed by analysis and operational constraints |

## Decision boundaries

There is no universal success score. Statistical evidence, practical value, guardrails, feasibility, and stakeholder judgment are presented separately. “Inconclusive,” “redesign,” and “this design cannot answer the question” are valid outcomes.
